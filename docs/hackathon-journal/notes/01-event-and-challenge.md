## The Mystery Stack draw
We picked the **Mystery Stack** route and drew three cards (the photo of the cards is not included in this vault):

| Ingredient | Card | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| Tech/Tool | **Phone motion** | Tilting, shaking or moving the phone must be a *core* way to play |
| Theme | **Rivalry** | Pit people against each other: friendly competition, bragging rights |
| Constraint | **Must work for a group of 4+** | At least four people playing together |

**Judged on:** functionality **and** creative use of all three ingredients.

### How Skill Issue hits each card
| Card | How the design answers it |
|---|---|
| Phone motion | The Runner **tilts** to walk, **twists** to sprint and **flicks** to jump or crouch. The Gunner **turns** the phone to aim. Buttons only handle discrete actions (fire, aim, reload, switch). |
| Rivalry | Red vs Blue on one TV, split screen, so you can see the other team's half. End screen with a winner reveal and a match MVP. |
| 4+ players | Two teams × two roles. The game *needs* four phones to play properly (a Player Count setting allows fewer for testing). |

> [!WARNING]
> **Lesson from the first mockup**
> The very first concept image used **touch joysticks**. That would have failed the phone-motion card, because motion has to be core, not optional. We switched to tilt and gyro within the first hour. See [18 Decision Log](18-decision-log.md) #4.

## MLH × DEV Writing Challenge
MLH acquired DEV (dev.to) in Feb 2026 and runs a **monthly writing challenge** for projects built at MLH hackathons ([rules](https://dev.to/mlh-hackathon)).

**Requirements**
- The project was built at an MLH hackathon.
- Publish a DEV post inside the monthly window (Eastern Time). For October: **by Oct 31**.
- Use the official template: **What I Built · Demo · Partner Technologies · Hackathon Experience**.
- Tags: `#mlhacks` (required), plus `devchallenge` and `hackathon`.
- Sign in with the **MLH account linked to the hackathon registration**.

**Prize:** $100 per selected project. DEV editors pick at least one each month.

**Judging:** technical substance · originality · clear storytelling · community popularity (sharing counts).

The first winner was praised for being open about its process, which is why these notes record **decisions and reasons**, not just features.
